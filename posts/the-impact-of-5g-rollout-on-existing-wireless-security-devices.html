The Impact of 5G Rollout on Existing Wireless Security Devices

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: The 5G rollout primarily impacts legacy wireless security devices operating on shared frequency bands, potentially causing signal interference or desensitization. Modern 5G signals can overlap with older Wi-Fi or proprietary RF frequencies, leading to connection drops. Upgrading to dual-band equipment or moving to hardwired backhauls is recommended for continued system reliability.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Radio Frequency (RF) congestion is the central issue facing homeowners as 5G infrastructure expands across the UK. Many older wireless security devices, including PIR sensors and doorbells, operate on frequency ranges that may face increased noise floor levels due to 5G deployment.

As an installer, I advise performing a comprehensive RF site survey before assuming your intermittent connectivity is a hardware fault. If your site is in an area with high 5G density, switching to 5GHz or 6GHz Wi-Fi bands or implementing a physical network backbone is often necessary to regain stability.

Always check your device's operating frequency against local 5G spectrum allocations to ensure long-term compatibility.

Mitigating Signal Interference and Instability

Bandwidth saturation caused by modern cellular networks can inadvertently lead to packet loss in wireless CCTV systems. When your security cameras compete for airtime with high-speed 5G traffic, transmission latency can spike, causing your NVR to lose frames or experience recording gaps.

To combat this, professional installers should configure Quality of Service (QoS) rules on the local router to prioritize security-related traffic. Alternatively, utilizing shielded Cat6A cabling for critical camera runs effectively creates a 'hardened' communication path, completely insulating your surveillance feed from external 5G cellular interference.

Best practice & Future-proofing your security

Matter interoperability standards are becoming the baseline for modern security, offering improved local control that is less dependent on unstable wide-area wireless connections. By transitioning to a local network architecture, you reduce the risk of environmental 5G noise disrupting your critical security alerts.

Compliance with current data protection regulations also demands a stable connection; if your security system is frequently offline due to signal interference, you risk gaps in surveillance coverage that could invalidate insurance claims. Prioritizing robust, hardwired infrastructure now ensures your property remains protected regardless of future cellular developments.
