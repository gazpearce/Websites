Securing Smart Locks Against Relay Attacks: A Technical Deep Dive

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: Smart locks are vulnerable to relay attacks where criminals amplify the signal between a key fob and the lock to gain unauthorized entry. To secure your smart home, disable auto-unlock features, use NFC-based authentication, and keep firmware updated to ensure modern encryption protocols are active.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Relay attack kits have become a significant threat to UK homeowners, as these devices can bridge the distance between a digital key fob and a smart lock, tricking the system into believing the owner is standing right at the door. By intercepting and re-transmitting low-frequency signals, attackers can bypass security without physical force.

To mitigate this risk, installation specialists recommend disabling 'Auto-Unlock' features that rely solely on Bluetooth signal strength. Instead, I suggest implementing geofencing combined with a secondary biometric confirmation, ensuring the lock only engages when the mobile device is physically present at the threshold.

Always store key fobs in a faraday pouch when not in use to physically block the transmission of radio signals to potential thieves.

Technical Countermeasures and Signal Hardening

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) encryption must be strictly managed to prevent interception, as older iterations of these protocols lacked the robust handshake security found in modern hardware. Manufacturers are now shifting towards secure 'Time-of-Flight' (ToF) distance measurements to verify the proximity of the digital key.

When configuring your smart system, prioritize locks that utilize AES-128 or higher encryption standards. Ensure your installation includes a robust network audit to identify any outdated protocols that might be acting as a gateway for signal spoofing.

Best practice & System Compliance

Network segmentation is essential for any modern smart home; isolating your smart locks on a separate Virtual Local Area Network (VLAN) prevents lateral movement if another, less-secure IoT device is compromised. Compliance with industry standards like those suggested by the SIA ensures your security hardware meets the latest resistance protocols.

Regular firmware updates are not optional; they often contain critical patches for known relay vulnerabilities. Establish a maintenance schedule to check for these updates, and always opt for professional-grade systems that guarantee long-term security support.
