Why this topic matters & Core context

Hardwired IP cameras are the gold standard for shared entrances, providing the reliability and image clarity necessary for identifying visitors or potential intruders in communal areas. Unlike wireless alternatives, a physical data connection ensures constant, uninterrupted recording which is vital when monitoring high-traffic residential lobbies or shared gateways.

To ensure a seamless installation, we focus on strategic mounting positions that cover ingress and egress points without infringing on private dwelling thresholds. By utilizing enterprise-grade NVRs, you ensure that footage remains secure, accessible for evidence, and protected against tampering.

Always conduct a site survey to identify light pollution and glare points before fixing cameras to communal walls.

Planning for Compliance and Privacy

GDPR-compliant surveillance is a non-negotiable aspect of any installation within a block of flats or shared housing. Homeowners and building managers must provide clear signage indicating that CCTV is in operation and limit the field of view strictly to communal spaces to avoid violating privacy rights.

We recommend configuring privacy masking on your recording software to black out areas belonging to individual flats. This professional approach mitigates legal disputes and maintains transparency between building management and residents.

Durability and Environmental Standards

IK10-rated housings are essential for equipment installed in publicly accessible areas, as they provide robust protection against intentional vandalism or accidental impacts. Because shared entrances are often exposed to varying weather conditions and high foot traffic, choosing hardware built to these durability standards ensures your security infrastructure remains operational for years.

Integrating high-quality weatherproofing with proper cable shielding prevents common failures associated with moisture and dust ingress. By investing in resilient hardware now, you avoid costly maintenance callouts and the potential for security gaps.
