Securing Smart Doorbells Against Unauthorized Access and Hijacking

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: Securing a smart doorbell requires a multi-layered approach: enforcing robust, unique passwords, enabling multi-factor authentication (MFA), isolating devices on a dedicated VLAN, and performing regular firmware updates. By treating your doorbell as a network gateway rather than a simple gadget, you significantly mitigate the risk of hijacking.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Network-connected IoT devices are frequently the weakest entry point into a home, often serving as a gateway for malicious actors to gain broader network access. Many homeowners underestimate that a simple smart doorbell can be the bridge between an external threat and their private home Wi-Fi infrastructure.

To prevent hijacking, we recommend segmenting your IoT devices using a dedicated VLAN, which prevents them from communicating directly with your main computers or sensitive data storage. Furthermore, ensuring that the doorbell uses encrypted communication protocols is a non-negotiable step for any professional installation.

Always disable 'UPnP' (Universal Plug and Play) on your router to stop devices from automatically opening security ports to the internet.

Hardening Authentication and Access

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) represents the most critical barrier against unauthorized account access, rendering stolen passwords largely ineffective. Even if a third party manages to obtain your login credentials, they will be blocked by the second verification step typically sent to your smartphone.

Beyond MFA, you must ensure all default administrative passwords are changed during the initial setup. I consistently advise my clients to use a dedicated password manager to generate and store complex, unique credentials for every device in the home.

Firmware Hygiene and Network Best Practices

Firmware management is the process of patching known vulnerabilities that developers discover after the product has hit the market. Hackers frequently target devices running legacy firmware, so enabling auto-updates or scheduling monthly manual checks is essential for long-term security.

Compliance with modern data privacy standards also requires that you periodically audit your application permissions. Ensure that you have revoked access for any third-party integrations or temporary users that no longer require a live view of your property.
