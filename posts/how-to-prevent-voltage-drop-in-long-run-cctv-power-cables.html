How to Prevent Voltage Drop in Long-Run CCTV Power Cables

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: Voltage drop occurs when cable resistance causes a loss of power over distance. To prevent it, use thicker gauge copper cabling (lower AWG), increase your supply voltage to compensate, or deploy a local power supply near the camera. Always calculate total current draw versus cable length before installation.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Ohmic resistance is the primary culprit behind intermittent CCTV failures, where the physical resistance of copper wire turns energy into heat rather than delivering it to the camera. If your power cables are undersized for the distance, you will experience flickering night vision, constant reboots, or complete camera failure once the IR LEDs trigger.

To mitigate these issues, always conduct a site survey to determine the total cable run and the camera's peak power consumption. By selecting high-quality, solid-core copper cabling instead of CCA (Copper Clad Aluminium), you can significantly reduce resistance and ensure a stable 12V supply reaches your device.

Always avoid Copper Clad Aluminium (CCA) cables for CCTV power runs as their high resistance makes them prone to severe voltage drop.

Technical Solutions for Long-Distance Power

Voltage adjustment at the power supply unit is a highly effective way to counteract drop over longer runs. Professional-grade power supplies often include a small trim potentiometer, allowing you to boost the output voltage slightly—typically to 13.5V—which ensures that the required 12V arrives at the camera end after the inevitable loss.

Alternatively, for exceptionally long distances, implementing a 24V AC power system or dedicated 24V DC to 12V DC converters at the camera end is recommended. This method allows you to transmit power at a higher voltage, drastically reducing the current draw and the resulting drop across the cable.

Best practice & Compliance for Professional Installs

System integrity depends on matching your power cable gauge (AWG) to the current requirement of the specific camera model. Installing a heavier gauge cable provides a larger conductor surface, which lowers the overall resistance and keeps the voltage drop within the safe operational margin defined by the manufacturer.

Adhering to these planning rules is critical for reliability and insurance compliance. By properly documenting your cable specifications and confirming voltage at the camera end using a multimeter, you ensure a robust installation that meets industry standards and avoids future maintenance issues.
