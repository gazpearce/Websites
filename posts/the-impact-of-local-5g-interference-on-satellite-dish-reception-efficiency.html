Direct Answer: Local 5G signals operating in the 700MHz frequency range can overlap with satellite TV broadcast frequencies, causing signal saturation in the LNB. This interference leads to pixelation, audio drops, or total loss of service. Installing a 5G-ready filter or upgrading to high-shielded cabling often resolves these reception efficiency issues.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Radio Frequency (RF) interference has become a significant headache for UK homeowners as the deployment of 5G infrastructure continues to densify. Because 5G masts frequently use frequencies adjacent to those processed by satellite Low Noise Block downconverters (LNBs), the high-power mobile signals can easily overwhelm the sensitive components of a standard dish setup.

To mitigate this, it is essential to conduct a professional site survey to determine if your property is within the proximity of a 5G base station. We often recommend installing a 5G band-pass filter between the LNB and the cable run to suppress unwanted signals before they reach your receiver, ensuring your signal-to-noise ratio remains within optimal parameters.

Always ensure your satellite cable is properly grounded to prevent external signal ingress and potential static buildup.

Identifying and Mitigating Signal Saturation

Signal saturation occurs when the sheer volume of out-of-band energy from 5G transmitters forces the LNB into a non-linear state, effectively blinding it to the weaker satellite signal. This is most common in urban areas where high-gain 5G antennas are mounted on rooftops or lamp posts directly in the line of sight of your satellite dish.

During installation, technicians must prioritize the use of high-quality, fully shielded coaxial cables. By replacing legacy cabling with high-screening standards (Class A+), you can create an effective Faraday cage effect that rejects electromagnetic interference before it can disrupt your digital viewing experience.

Best practice / compliance section 3

Compliance with Ofcom guidelines regarding spectral interference is a critical aspect of modern AV installation. While many newer LNB models now include built-in 5G filtering, retrofitting older systems requires a methodical approach to ensure that your setup complies with current electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) standards.

For optimal performance, always position your dish as far as physically possible from mobile antennas and utilize high-quality connectors to prevent signal leakage. Professional alignment tools are necessary to maximize gain, ensuring that the desired satellite signal remains strong enough to overcome even minor levels of ambient interference.
