Direct Answer: To maximise Starlink speed, ensure a clear, 360-degree view of the sky for the dish, replace the standard router with a high-performance mesh system, and use hardwired Ethernet backhauls. Why this topic matters & Core context

Clear line-of-sight is the most significant factor in maintaining a consistent Starlink connection for demanding professional environments. Any minor obstruction, such as tree branches or building eaves, will cause intermittent packet loss, leading to disruptive lag during critical business video calls.

To address this, ensure your dish is mounted with a professional-grade bracket that provides complete sky clearance. I always recommend performing a site survey with the Starlink app to identify 'dead zones' before committing to a permanent location for your hardware.

Use a professional sky-scan tool to check for minor tree growth patterns, as even small branches can degrade signal during high-wind conditions.

Optimising Network Hardware

High-performance routers are essential because the standard Starlink router often struggles to manage the overhead of multiple concurrent work devices and VPN traffic. By using the Starlink Ethernet adapter, you can bypass the factory-supplied equipment and connect to a professional-grade, tri-band mesh Wi-Fi system.

Configuring a dedicated VLAN or using a managed switch allows you to prioritise traffic for your office hardware. Implementing Quality of Service (QoS) rules will ensure that your video conferencing bandwidth remains reserved even when other household devices are streaming or updating software.

Best practice & Physical cabling

Shielded CAT6 cabling is the gold standard for connecting your office devices directly to the router to avoid the inherent instability of wireless signals. For external runs between a main house and a garden office, always utilise outdoor-rated, shielded twisted pair cables to prevent electromagnetic interference and potential ground loops.

Always ensure your installation adheres to local electrical safety standards and that your data infrastructure is properly labelled for future maintenance. A clean, structured cabling cabinet not only looks professional but also prevents accidental damage and facilitates quick troubleshooting during network audits.
