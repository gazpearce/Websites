Can You Legally Use CCTV to Monitor Public Footpaths in the UK?

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: Yes, you can legally use CCTV on your property, but you must ensure the primary focus remains your own land. Monitoring public footpaths is generally permitted if it is unavoidable for security, provided you inform the public and comply with UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 requirements.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Privacy masking is the technical cornerstone of balancing domestic security with the rights of the public. While homeowners have the right to protect their property, capturing images of pedestrians on public footpaths often strays into legal territory that requires careful configuration.

To remain compliant, I always advise clients to position their lenses to exclude as much public space as possible. If a wider field of view is essential for coverage, modern software settings allow you to black out specific areas of the frame to protect the privacy of those passing by.

Always display clear, visible signage if your camera captures any area beyond your property boundary to notify the public of surveillance.

Understanding your legal obligations

Data Protection Act 2018 standards govern how you store, process, and delete the footage you capture from public areas. Operating a system that monitors public space transforms your domestic setup into a data controller operation, necessitating strict adherence to transparency and data minimization principles.

You should implement a clear retention policy, ensuring footage is not held indefinitely without a specific, justified reason. Additionally, if a member of the public requests access to footage where they appear, you are legally obligated under Subject Access Requests to provide that data within the statutory timeframe.

Best practice & installation compliance

Professional CCTV installation involves not just hardware selection, but a thorough risk assessment to justify the use of surveillance. By documenting why you believe your security measures are necessary and proportionate, you provide a clear defense should you be challenged regarding privacy concerns.

Regular maintenance of your NVR settings, such as auditing user access and verifying that your motion detection triggers are not constantly activated by public foot traffic, is essential. Keeping your system optimized for your property's specific boundaries is the most effective way to stay on the right side of the law.
