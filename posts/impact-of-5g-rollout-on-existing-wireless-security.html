Impact of 5G Rollout on Existing Wireless Security

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: The 5G rollout introduces potential signal interference with older wireless security frequencies and increases the attack surface for connected devices. To maintain robust protection, homeowners should transition to hardwired connections, update firmware, and isolate IoT devices on dedicated network VLANs.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Radio Frequency (RF) interference is the primary technical concern as 5G infrastructure expands across UK urban and residential areas. Because many older wireless alarm systems and CCTV cameras operate on overlapping spectrums, the increase in signal density can lead to packet loss, connection drops, and reduced system reliability.

As an installer, I advise clients to treat wireless security as a supplementary layer rather than a core foundation. If your system relies heavily on 2.4GHz or 5GHz wireless bands, conducting a professional RF spectrum survey is essential to identify potential points of failure before connectivity issues compromise your home security.

Always prioritize hardwired PoE (Power over Ethernet) connections for mission-critical security cameras to bypass wireless congestion entirely.

Cybersecurity and Network Vulnerabilities

Network segmentation is the most effective defense against the heightened security risks brought by the rapid adoption of 5G-enabled IoT devices. With more devices connecting to high-speed 5G gateways, malicious actors have more entry points to scan for vulnerabilities within your local home network.

To mitigate these risks, homeowners must isolate security hardware from guest networks and personal devices using VLANs. Furthermore, ensuring that all NVRs and cameras have the latest security patches installed is a non-negotiable step to protect against modern remote exploitation attempts.

Best practice & Compliance for 2024

BS8418 compliance principles remain the benchmark for high-end security installations, regardless of the underlying connectivity medium. Moving toward a professional-grade setup means moving away from plug-and-play wireless gadgets that often lack robust encryption standards.

When planning your security infrastructure, consider future-proofing with shielded cabling and professional-grade network switches. Staying compliant not only improves the longevity of your security equipment but also ensures that your footage remains protected and your alarms remain trigger-reliable in an increasingly dense wireless environment.
