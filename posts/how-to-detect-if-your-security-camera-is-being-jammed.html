How to Detect if Your Security Camera is Being Jammed

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: To detect jamming, monitor your NVR for 'video loss' alerts, sudden signal drops, or recurring connectivity errors. Use a Wi-Fi analyzer to check for suspicious high-frequency interference patterns. If your cameras go offline simultaneously during motion events, it is likely a deliberate signal interference attack.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Wireless CCTV systems are increasingly targeted by sophisticated burglars using portable RF jammers to disrupt signal transmission. By flooding the frequency band your camera uses—typically 2.4GHz or 5GHz—an intruder can effectively blind your monitoring station while they gain unauthorized access to your property.

As an installer, I advise clients that the most effective way to identify this threat is by establishing a baseline for your network performance. If you notice persistent latency or intermittent signal loss that correlates with specific times, it is time to audit your system for potential electronic countermeasures.

Always enable 'Video Loss' notification alerts in your NVR settings to receive immediate push updates if a camera feed is disrupted.

Identifying Signal Interference

Radio Frequency (RF) interference manifests as a clean break in your feed, often accompanied by frozen images or 'No Signal' errors on your NVR display. Unlike simple hardware failure, jamming typically affects multiple cameras at once if they share the same wireless access point or frequency band.

You can conduct a basic scan using a spectrum analyzer or even free Wi-Fi analyzer apps to detect unusual noise floors in your area. If you see massive, sustained spikes of interference that do not match the expected patterns of your neighbors' routers, your home network is being targeted.

Best practice & Hardening your system

Hardwired CAT6 cabling is the gold standard for high-security installations, effectively removing the vulnerability associated with over-the-air signal transmission. By migrating from wireless to a Power-over-Ethernet (PoE) setup, you eliminate the possibility of remote jamming and gain superior reliability for your data streams.

In addition to cabling, ensure that your NVR is stored in a locked, tamper-proof cabinet to prevent physical access to your storage media. Following these infrastructure standards not only secures your footage but ensures that your home security remains robust against even the most persistent intruders.
