Active Infrared (AIR) Anti-Masking Transmitter & Receiver Diode Diagnostics

Effective intruder detection relies critically on the integrity of sensor functionality, particularly in high-security environments. **Active Infrared (AIR)** anti-masking technology represents a cornerstone in preventing sophisticated circumvention attempts against perimeter and volumetric detectors. Understanding its intricate diode diagnostics is paramount for NSI/SSAIB compliant installations.

### Fundamentals of AIR Anti-Masking Diode Architecture

**Active Infrared (AIR)** anti-masking systems typically employ a dedicated array of pulsed infrared emitters and corresponding **photovoltaic diode** receivers. These components are strategically positioned within the detector housing, forming a precisely monitored, close-proximity infrared curtain. This intricate design allows for continuous verification of the sensor's optical surface integrity.

The transmitter diodes emit a continuous or pulsed IR beam, while the receiver diodes constantly monitor the reflected or direct signal strength. Any significant, sustained change in this signal, indicative of a masking attempt, triggers an alarm condition. This methodology ensures the detector remains operational and resistant to sabotage.

### Signal Attenuation and Masking Detection

Masking attempts, such as applying opaque spray paint or positioning an obstructing material directly over the sensor lens, critically disrupt the **optical path integrity**. This disruption leads to a measurable **signal attenuation** in the received IR energy. Advanced systems employ sophisticated algorithms to differentiate genuine masking from environmental fluctuations.

The system's firmware analyses the **signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)** and compares the attenuated signal against a pre-calibrated baseline and a defined **attenuation threshold**. Exceeding this threshold for a specified duration registers a masking event, generating a local tamper alarm and remote notification to the Alarm Receiving Centre (ARC). This proactive detection is vital for **Grade 3** and **Grade 4 security** applications.

### Spray-Paint Detection Mechanisms

Spray paint, especially certain spectral absorption types, poses a significant threat by rapidly rendering a conventional sensor blind without triggering a traditional tamper switch. **AIR anti-masking** specifically addresses this by monitoring changes in the detector's near-field environment. The rapid deposition of opaque material on the lens causes an immediate, sharp drop in the detected infrared signal.

This sudden and persistent reduction in IR energy, which cannot be attributed to normal ambient light changes or environmental dust, is precisely what the **photovoltaic diode** array is designed to detect. The system intelligently differentiates between slow degradation due to environmental factors and the abrupt onset of a masking event. This ensures robust detection against even quick-drying aerosols.

### Critical Optical Path Checking and Compliance

Regular and diagnostic optical path checking is not merely good practice; it is a fundamental requirement under standards such as **BS EN 50131-1** and **PD 6662:2017**. This involves verifying the clear line of sight for both the primary detection elements and the anti-masking array. Any physical obstructions, smudges, or component degradation must be rectified immediately.
