How to Clean and Maintain Your Security Camera Lenses

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: To clean security camera lenses effectively, use a soft, lint-free microfibre cloth and a specialised optical cleaning solution. Gently wipe in a circular motion to remove dust, spiderwebs, or residue. Avoid household glass cleaners, as they can damage delicate lens coatings and cause permanent glare or blurriness.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Hikvision dome cameras and other outdoor surveillance units are frequently exposed to the harsh UK elements, leading to dust, salt, and grime buildup. This accumulation degrades image quality significantly, often causing infrared reflection during night vision mode.

As a certified installer, I always recommend a bi-annual maintenance schedule to prevent permanent pitting on the lens glass. Proper physical maintenance ensures that your NVR captures crisp evidence rather than obscured shapes.

Always power down or disconnect your cameras before cleaning to prevent accidental damage to the sensors or internal circuitry.

Professional Cleaning Techniques

Microfibre lens cloths are the gold standard for removing debris without introducing microscopic scratches to the lens surface. You should never use paper towels or harsh rags, as these can strip away the anti-reflective coatings applied by manufacturers.

When dealing with stubborn dried-on residue or bird droppings, use a small amount of lens cleaning fluid sprayed onto the cloth—never directly onto the glass itself. This prevents liquid from seeping into the camera housing and causing water ingress or electrical shorts.

Best practice & Compliance

IP67-rated enclosures are designed to be weather-resistant, yet the seals can degrade over time if not inspected during cleaning cycles. Regular maintenance allows you to check for moisture condensation inside the housing, which is a common indicator of a failing rubber gasket.

Maintaining your equipment to manufacturer standards is essential for legal compliance, especially if the footage is intended to be used as evidence. Keeping your field of view clear ensures you stay within privacy guidelines while maximizing the reliability of your deterrent system.
