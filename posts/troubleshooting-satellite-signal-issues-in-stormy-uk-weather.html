Direct Answer: Satellite signal loss during storms is typically caused by heavy rain attenuation or wind-induced dish misalignment. To troubleshoot, ensure the LNB is dry, check for physical obstructions like debris, and verify that the mounting bracket remains secure. If issues persist, professional alignment using a digital spectrum analyser is recommended.

Why this topic matters & Core context

Rain fade is the primary technical phenomenon responsible for signal degradation during heavy downpours, where moisture effectively absorbs or scatters the high-frequency microwave signals from the satellite. In the UK's unpredictable climate, even a slight shift in your dish's orientation can lead to pixelation or total loss of service during high-wind events.

To mitigate these issues, we ensure that every mounting bracket is reinforced with weather-resistant hardware and that cable connections are sealed with self-amalgamating tape. As an installer, I find that proactive maintenance of the dish assembly is far more effective than reactive repairs after a storm has passed.

Always disconnect your receiver from the mains before inspecting outdoor cabling to prevent static discharge or short-circuit damage.

Identifying Physical and Atmospheric Faults

Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) metrics provide the most accurate indication of whether your dish is physically misaligned or suffering from weather-based interference. By accessing your satellite receiver's signal diagnostic menu, you can determine if the drop is localized to specific channels or an entire frequency band.

Standard industry practice involves checking the 'dish skew' and elevation settings, which can be nudged by gusty winds. If your signal strength fluctuates wildly while the weather is calm, the fault likely lies within your LNB (Low-Noise Block) degrading or water ingress occurring in the coax cable connections.

Best practice for long-term reliability

BS EN 60728-11 standards outline the mandatory safety and earthing requirements for satellite installations to protect against lightning surges and structural stress. Adherence to these regulations is critical, as it ensures that your installation can withstand the mechanical loads imposed by severe UK winter storms.

For optimal performance, I recommend using high-quality copper-clad steel or pure copper coaxial cabling with compression connectors rather than standard crimp types. Regular inspection of the mast fixings and applying a thin coat of silicone grease to LNB connectors can prevent oxidation and water ingress, ensuring your system remains robust throughout the year.
