Direct Answer: Performing a DIY network audit involves scanning for open ports, identifying connected devices, verifying firmware updates, and isolating IoT equipment via VLANs. Why this topic matters & Core context

Network vulnerability assessment is the cornerstone of modern home security, especially when you have high-definition CCTV systems integrated into your domestic infrastructure. Without a routine audit, your connected devices become easy entry points for malicious actors scanning for common weaknesses in residential routers.

To perform a effective audit, start by logging into your router and documenting every active IP address on your network. Compare these against your known devices, such as cameras and smart hubs, to identify any unrecognized hardware that may have been silently added or breached.

Always ensure your router's firmware is updated to the latest manufacturer release before starting your audit to ensure security patches are active.

Identifying and Closing Security Gaps

Port forwarding is the most common vulnerability found in DIY security camera installations, as it essentially leaves your front door open to the public internet. During your audit, navigate to your router’s ‘NAT/Port Forwarding’ section and disable any rules that are not absolutely essential for your system’s operation.

Instead of port forwarding, use secure alternatives like a hardware VPN or a secure cloud-based relay provided by your camera manufacturer. This prevents external bots from brute-forcing their way into your NVR or camera login portals, which remain frequent targets for automated attacks.

Best practice / compliance section 3

Network segmentation is the gold standard for separating sensitive traffic from less secure Internet of Things (IoT) devices. By creating a dedicated VLAN for your security cameras, you ensure that even if a smart lightbulb or low-cost sensor is compromised, the attacker cannot pivot to your NVR or computer systems.

Always adhere to the principle of least privilege, ensuring that your devices only communicate with the servers required for their core functionality. Periodic audits, paired with robust password policies, ensure your residential network remains compliant with modern cybersecurity best practices.
