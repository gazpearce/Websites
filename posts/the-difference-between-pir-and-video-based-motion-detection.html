The Difference Between PIR and Video-Based Motion Detection

📅 Published: May 2026 ✅ Last updated: 23 May 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🇬🇧 UK Home Guide
← All Guides
Direct Answer: PIR (Passive Infrared) sensors detect heat signature changes, offering high accuracy for outdoor environments. Video-based motion detection uses software analysis of pixel changes across frames, which is flexible but prone to false alarms from weather or shadows. Professional installations often use a hybrid approach for maximum reliability.

Why this topic matters & Core context

PIR motion sensors are the backbone of reliable outdoor security, operating by detecting the rapid change in infrared heat signatures within a specific field of view. Understanding how these differ from standard camera-based algorithms is critical to preventing the frustration of constant false notifications.

When planning your layout, PIR sensors provide hardware-level precision that is rarely affected by swaying trees or changing light levels. Conversely, video-based analytics depend on the camera's processing power to register pixel shifts, making them better suited for indoor monitoring where environmental conditions remain stable.

Always mount PIR sensors at the manufacturer's recommended height to ensure the detection zone is optimized for human movement rather than pets.

Technical operation and deployment

Video-based motion detection functions by monitoring contrast and movement between sequential frames in a recorded stream. This method allows for 'motion masking,' where you can draw specific zones on your screen to trigger alerts only when activity occurs in high-risk areas.

Installing these systems requires careful consideration of ambient lighting and frame rates to avoid motion blur, which can lead to missed events. In complex exterior environments, I always recommend supplementing standard video detection with external hardware sensors to verify the threat level before an alert is sent.

Best practice and system integration

Hybrid security architectures represent the pinnacle of modern residential protection, combining the physical sensitivity of PIR with the investigative power of high-resolution video. By linking a physical PIR alarm to a camera event, you ensure that recording only begins when a genuine heat-source intrusion is confirmed.

Compliance with UK privacy standards, such as the DPA 2018, necessitates that your detection zones are strictly limited to your own property boundaries. Proper calibration of sensitivity settings not only improves system reliability but also keeps you in line with 'good neighbour' policies regarding CCTV coverage.
